The baseline these numbers come from is one live run of 500 logs (400 normal / 100 anomalous) against a real `mistral:7b`: parsing scored 58.6% field accuracy overall (ssh 62.5%, http 43.3%, ftp 66.7%, dns 40.0%, packages 56.25%) with `failed_lines: 0` everywhere, and the run took 2,286.0s (38.1 min), 76.6% of it parsing (1,751.3s of 2,286.0s). Behavioral detection caught all 12 engineered ssh anomalies with zero false positives (precision, recall and F1 all 1.0), while http — 12 clean entities, zero engineered anomalies — had 4 flagged (33%), and ftp's 8 clean entities had none. Supply-chain detection caught 2 of 8 engineered threats (precision 1.0, recall 0.25, F1 0.4): `flask` and `django` were caught on size/timing as correctly-spelled names, and all six misses (`reqeusts`, `nunpy`, `numpi`, `djngo`, `pandaas`, `scipy2`) depended on typosquat matching; a follow-up re-parse of just the 40 threat lines still returned malformed JSON on 2 of 8 batches after 4 retries. Adversarial coverage was 78.7% over 15 MITRE techniques with 5 generated evasion variants each, with T1040 (Network Sniffing) at 0% and T1110, T1048 and T1195 at 60%.

Those results ride on fixed parameters: each entity's events are cut into windows of 5 events, summarised as 8 features (`failed_login_ratio`, `login_frequency`, `unique_users`, `unique_ports`, `connection_duration_mean`, `bytes_transferred_mean`, `geographic_changes`, `off_hours_activity`); a population isolation forest of 200 trees with `max_samples=min(n, 256)`; a flag threshold of `anomaly_threshold=0.6`; a median/MAD gate using `0.6745 * |x - median| / MAD` where two or more features must exceed 3.5 before the population branch is trusted; a per-entity forest only for entities with 3 or more windows (11+ events); and supply-chain weights of 0.30 (typosquat, ~106 known packages), 0.20 (a rare source seen fewer than twice), 0.25 (a size jump beyond mean + 2 std) and 0.15 (off-hours installs), flagging at 0.4 or more. Parsing ran on qwen2.5:7b at temperature 0.1 with schema-constrained output; explaining ran on mistral:7b at temperature 0.5, only for flagged items.

Later sessions re-measured on a real-format corpus. Session 39 built 704 lines (420 normal, 261 anomalous, 23 noise; ssh 167, http 147, ftp 105, dns 163, packages 122). Session 40 ran the pipeline live against it in 1020 s, with overall parsing at 85.9% (ssh 90.9%, http 98.9%, ftp 75.1%, dns 100%, packages 53.8% and 8 failed lines) and a template-cache hit rate of 40.0%; detection scored ssh F1 0.933, ftp 0.889, and 0.0 for http and supply chain because those two detectors crashed, while feeding the detectors ground truth instead of parser output gave behavioral 1.0 and supply-chain F1 0.5 (2 of 6). Session 41 then swept the detectors alone, with no LLM, over 62 cells: recall was 1.00 in every behavioral cell, ssh corpus false positives ran 1.8% of entities at 50,000 (precision 0.54) against http's 0.09% (precision 0.96), clean-window gate statistics stayed flat (1.63% / 1.91% / 1.77% of ssh clean windows passing the two-feature gate at N=1k/10k/50k), 50,000 per-entity forests cost 4.06 GB and 24 min of `fit()` at a constant 83 KB and 29 ms each, and supply-chain detection at 50,000 distinct packages took 10.0 s (~200 µs/package, 26 MB).

Two documents disagree with each other on numbers. Session 9's summary reports parsing as 76.6% of a 2,286.0s wall clock, while Session 7's bottleneck analysis reports parsing as 70.3% of 2,609.1s (1,833.8s), with `dns` at 332.6s and 18.1% of that pipeline's wall time against Session 9's 52.2s and 2.3% — the two runs used different corpora (Session 7: 250 ssh / 100 dns / 50 http / 50 ftp / 50 packages; Session 9: 300 / 60 / 40 / 20 / 80), so the shares are not like-for-like. Separately, the blog series' technique table carries rates the results JSON does not: T1003 62.5% in the post against 1.0 in the JSON, T1059 50% against 0.8, and T1078, T1140 and T1036 at 75% against 0.8, 1.0 and 1.0 — both agree on the 78.7% overall figure and on T1040 at 0%.
